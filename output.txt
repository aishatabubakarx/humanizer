**SEC Scraps Key Crypto Rulemaking Session as a Halt in Regulation Wishes**

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission abruptly canceled an important Friday morning meeting where its three commissioners were scheduled to vote on the agency's first crypto-specific rules. The move will deal a major blow to a sector that desperately needs regulatory clarity. The announced August 15, 2026, cancellation comes just days after the Senate suspended a five-week hiatus for failing to advance the Clarity Act. Now, both the administrative and legislative means of crypto surveillance have been left in the dark. **Cancellation**

An official note released under the Sunshine Act and signed by SEC Secretary Vanessa Countryman offered little explanation: "the meeting has been canceled," it simply stated without giving a new date. The spokesperson later said that the hearing would be postponed to a later date due to an unexpected scheduling problem, but declined to elaborate. The SEC's original filing on August 10 had issued only a three-business day warning, with only one item on the agenda. The Commissioners were prepared to open up a number of exceptions to the public comment period. The purpose of these proposed rules was to create an "escape gate" for crypto startups to raise capital without fully following the traditional rules of securities supply – a path the founders have been looking for for years. Even if the vote had not immediately created binding rules, it would have marked a crucial moment: the agency's first assault on crypto-specific rule-making, going beyond the mere application of existing securities laws. **Regulatory Encryption: What Was on the Table**

The deferred rules, dubbed "Regulation Crypto," represented what many considered to be the SEC's first major regulatory order in the digital asset space. This ambitious framework was intended to make limited exceptions for cryptocurrency offerings. Its goal: to allow issuers to develop projects without full registration requirements, ultimately providing a pathway for a gradual exit from SEC regulation. SEC Chair Paul Atkins had previously advocated for this code as the cornerstone of the agency's digital asset program. In March, Atkins had drafted extensive guidelines for a possible safe haven. This protection could cover startups worth up to $5 million during their first four crypto trials, entrepreneurs raising up to $75 million through investment deals, and tokens whose creators had waived essential management efforts. Another "innovation exemption" that is yet to emerge would have allowed companies to experiment with blockchain-based stocks and similar products without meeting all of the SEC's disclosure requirements. CoinDesk reports that industry sources believe that the delay in this exemption may be tied to controversial discussions over the Clarity Act. **Clarity Act Stalls in the Senate**

The SEC's abrupt withdrawal comes after the Senate went on a five-week hiatus on Saturday for failing to advance the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, which is the industry's top enforcement priority. The bill is not expected to face its next procedural hurdle until September, and its chances of being passed this year look increasingly bleak. At Myriad, a popular prediction market, traders estimate the chances of the Clarity Act becoming law in 2026 by just 20%. Lobbyists argue that if the legislation is passed, it will eventually create new federal rules specifically for cryptocurrencies, which would provide businesses with a much-needed stable legal basis. However, the bill has faced numerous obstacles, largely due to fierce opposition from representatives of the banking industry and consumer protection organizations. Bipartisan negotiations with the White House have so far failed to reach the consensus required to remove the Senate's challenging 60-vote threshold. **Both routes are now stuck**

With both regulatory and legislative avenues now at a standstill, crypto companies are still bogged down in significant uncertainty about the legal status of their operations. The SEC's planned rules had been widely seen as an attempt to bridge the regulatory gap left by the Clarity Act for Senate negligence. But because the meeting has been postponed for the time being, the sector now has no legislative or administrative way forward. This latest setback comes despite a broader shift in SEC policy during the Trump administration. Under the leadership of Chairman Atkins, the agency has sharply reversed its course from its previous democratic leadership, which famously sued several crypto companies claiming that their tokens were unregistered securities. In fact, in 2025, the SEC quickly moved to strict crypto accounting guidelines and dropped high-profile lawsuits against major players like Coinbase and Binance. **CFTC continues its own path**

While the SEC's efforts are stumbled, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) is moving forward with its own initiatives. The CFTC is still on track to host the inaugural meeting of its innovation committee on August 20. On the agenda is a promising inaugural session: "Crypto's Regulatory Evolution From Uncertainty to Clarity". The Committee also intends to address the regulation of AI and prediction markets. However, it is important to remember that the CFTC committee is purely advisory and is limited to making recommendations rather than enforceable rules. **Market impact**

The news of the SEC postponement sent immediate vibrations in the crypto market. Concept stocks, including Bullish, Coinbase, and the UNI token, all declined after the announcement. Bitcoin, on the other hand, traded just under $63,000 on Saturday. This new wave of regulatory uncertainty only exacerbated the existing selling pressure that has kept the range of cryptocurrencies limited throughout August. Analysts suggest that the SEC's decision to postpone the "freedom to innovate" for tokenized securities dampened market optimism in particular. They reported that the delay was due to concerns from both the White House and major financial institutions about the regulatory basis behind the proposal.
